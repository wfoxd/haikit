"@haikit/auth": minor
---

New, optional package: `@haikit/auth`. `createAuth({ store, jwt })` says who is asking, from a verified JWT, and makes a `gate` for `nodeHandler` that lets each user use only the conversations they started; another user's is answered as one that doesn't exist. Tokens are either issued by `signIn`, after the app's own login, as an HS256 JWT in a `__Host-haikit_token` cookie (`jwt.secret`, rotatable), or issued by the app's identity provider and checked against its JWKS (`jwt.jwks`, `issuer`, `audience`), sent as the cookie or `Authorization: Bearer`. The gate refuses a POST that isn't JSON (415), and one carrying the cookie from another site (403). `auth.handler()` serves `GET /auth/me`, which renews a cookie past half its life, and `POST /auth/sign-out`. A tool learns its user with `auth.ownerOf(ctx.conversationId)`, in a wake turn too. `revoke(userId)` refuses every sign-in before it: a token `signIn` issued carries its sign-in time as `auth_time`, which a renewal keeps, so a renewal can't outlive a revocation. `clockToleranceMs` must be a finite number, at least 0, `userClaim` can't be a claim every token sets for itself, such as `iss`, and a `__Host-` or `__Secure-` cookie name needs `secure`. Every token must have an `exp`. A cookie's origin is checked against the request's `Host` and `origins` only: `x-forwarded-host` isn't trusted. `memoryAuthStore()` keeps owners and revocations in one process, and `pgAuthStore(db)` from `@haikit/auth/postgres`, with `migrate` and `schema`, in Postgres.
